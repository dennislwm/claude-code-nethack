# Why do I keep dying?

## Introduction

This guide offers survival tips for NetHack beginners, focusing on early-game strategy and addressing common misconceptions. It assumes you've played several games and understand basic mechanics like opening doors. Readers unfamiliar with fundamental concepts should first consult the NetHack Guidebook.

## The one good game

The belief that winning NetHack requires exceptional luck is a myth. "If your survival depends on luck then you are following the wrong strategy." Most unavoidable deaths occur early, before you acquire necessary countermeasures. Expert players achieve ascension in approximately 80% of their games without receiving more fortunate drops than other players. Success comes from developing safe, consistent habits rather than relying on chance.

## Moving fast, typing slowly

**Never explore while Burdened or worse.** When Stressed, opponents attack twice for each of your moves, causing rapid health loss. Use diagonal movement keys rather than arrow keys to reach destinations faster. Diagonal movement reduces travel time significantly.

Type deliberately and avoid holding keys for autorepeat. When keys repeat automatically, you often stumble into danger faster than you can release the key. Use commands like G, g, and capital YUHJKLBN for directional movement until something interesting appears.

Rest using the period command (.) when healthy and no monsters are nearby. Play purposefully with specific goals: acquiring effective weapons, improving armor class, identifying magical items, or gaining resistances. This thoughtful approach improves gameplay rapidly.

## Traps

On dungeon level 1, every deadly trap contains an automatically generated corpse. This probability decreases 25% per level, so levels 5 and below never have auto-corpses.

If caught in a bear trap, escape five times faster by attempting diagonal movement. Beware of darts, arrows, and rocks on the ground—these may be traps. Rolling boulder traps cause 1d20 damage.

Poisoned darts and spiked pits can cause instant death. An alchemy smock provides early protection. Barbarians begin with poison resistance, while Monks gain it at level 3.

## Finding down

"Every room has a way out." Search with the S command along walls or corridor dead-ends. Finding a hidden door typically requires ten or more searches. If all doors are locked and you lack opening tools, kick them down—but this awakens sleeping monsters and triggers shop attacks.

"Every level has a way down." Look for large empty areas suggesting undiscovered rooms. Stairs may be hidden under items. Use the # terrain command to view maps without visual clutter. The underscore (>) command displays stairs despite overlying objects.

## Monsters too tough?

Monster difficulty depends on the average of your experience level and dungeon level. Venturing deeper generates increasingly powerful opponents. When encountering particularly dangerous levels or areas like the Oracle level, consider returning later when better equipped or more experienced.

## Drinking water

Don't drink. Your character survives entire games without consuming anything. Wasting potions of water through drinking is inefficient; convert them to holy water instead. Drinking from fountains is dangerous, potentially summoning hostile water nymphs, water demons, or moccasins.

## Eating

Two poisoning types exist. Food poisoning ("FoodPois") comes from consuming old corpses and always kills. Prevention is simple: "Eat your corpses fresh"—within 60 turns. Lichens, lizards, and ice-boxed corpses don't age. Undead creatures always carry food poisoning.

Regular poison comes from certain monster types like kobolds. It lowers attributes and HP but won't directly kill. "Corpses your pet dog or cat will eat are safe" with rare exceptions. Gain poison resistance as soon as possible for protection against poisoned pits, arrows, and similar threats.

Proper foods like eggs and tripe rations suit pets. Keep standard food for emergencies. You can also survive through prayer alone if necessary.

## Praying

The NetHack gods want help requests, not constant complaints. Prayer used sparingly helps escape tight situations. Wait 700–1400 turns between prayers—the longer interval the better. Sacrifice shortens this timeout and may grant gifts. Excessive prayer invites divine punishment including disintegration.

## Watching your pet

Pets reveal which corpses are safe through eating patterns. Horses refuse meat, helping identify vegetarian options. Pets also indicate cursed items: "Pets will step on cursed items only reluctantly, if at all." Drop equipment for pet-testing before wearing unidentified items.

Pets kill dangerous creatures like nymphs before they steal from you and can eliminate peaceful unicorns without divine consequence. Keep pets away from powerful enemies like watch captains and shopkeepers, or consider polymorphing them.

## Identifying

Never carelessly read, quaff, or zap unidentified items—this approach is suicidally dangerous.

**Armor, weapons, and amulets** are safe after curse-testing with pets. Few items autocurse, and side effects rarely prove life-threatening.

**Rings** are safe when curse-tested, except three types: conflict, polymorph, and teleportation. Avoid testing near shops or powerful pets. Remove rings immediately after one turn to minimize complications.

**Wands** are identifiable through engraving tests. Write Elbereth, then engrave with the wand to observe effects. Six wands produce no messages and require alternative identification. Never store wands that erase engravings in bags of holding unless absolutely certain they're teleportation or invisibility wands—cancellation wands destroy bags and contents.

**Potions** require careful identification. Clear potions are water. Oil potions glow when applied. Dip junk darts to discover polymorph and sickness potions. Dip unicorn horns to convert three harmful potions to water. Test sleep and paralysis potions with sleep resistance or free action rings. All other potions are safe if not cursed.

**Scrolls** use price identification. Drop scrolls at shops and multiply offers by 2 (or 3 for tourists) to find base prices. Identify scrolls are cheapest at base price 20. Light scrolls cost 50; enchant weapon costs 60; remove curse and enchant armor cost 80 each.

Monsters only read teleportation, create monster, and rarely earth scrolls. Teleportation scrolls appear only in closets. Sokoban's first level contains two earth scrolls. Scare monster scrolls sit under Sokoban prizes and cause peaceful creatures to flee.
